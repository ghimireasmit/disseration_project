Study design

What was evaluated

Research focus

The project studies a combined explainability and fairness audit for machine-learning fraud detection, considered in the context of the UK FCA Consumer Duty. It compares a logistic-regression baseline with a LightGBM model and examines outcomes across age, income, and employment groups.

Evaluation approach

  • Temporal evaluation: train on earlier months and test on months 6–7.
  • Imbalanced-class metrics include AUC-PR and recall at a 5% false-positive-rate target.
  • SHAP analyses explain global patterns and individual predictions.
  • Fairness metrics compare error rates across customer groups.

LightGBM has stronger AUC-PR and recall in this test, while its observed false-positive rate is higher than the 5% validation target. This illustrates why performance and customer impact need to be considered together.

Interpretation

Key takeaways

Performance is a trade-off

LightGBM improves AUC-PR and recall in this evaluation, but its test false-positive rate is above the validation target.

Explanations are not fairness guarantees

SHAP helps describe model contributions, but a transparent explanation alone does not prevent unequal error rates.

Removing features is not a complete fix

Disparities remain after excluding protected attributes, consistent with other features retaining group-related information.
